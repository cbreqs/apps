Product & Pricing Description

Leaf a Tip

A tipping platform for service providers in cannabis-adjacent businesses. Tips, not product.

TYPICAL LLC · leafatip.com

01What Leaf a Tip Is

Leaf a Tip is a digital tipping platform for service providers in cannabis-adjacent businesses — tour guides, budtenders, event staff, educators. It is not a cannabis sales platform. No product changes hands through Leaf a Tip. Customers use it to tip people for service they've already received, the same way they'd tip a bartender or a hairstylist.

The name carries the industry association on purpose, because that's who the platform serves. The transactions themselves are tips, full stop.

02How a Transaction Works

There are two distinct money movements here, and it matters to keep them apart:

  1. A customer buys a prepaid credit pack by card. This is the only card transaction in the system.
  2. They finish a tour, class, or service.
  3. They scan a QR code displayed by the guide or business, which opens that guide's Leaf a Tip page in their browser — no app install required.
  4. They pick a tip tier (or enter a custom amount) and spend credit they already bought. No card is charged at this step.
  5. The tip transfers to the service provider's own connected account.

All card activity is card-not-present, one-time, low dollar, and initiated entirely by the customer. Nobody is billed on a schedule and nothing ships.

Custom tip amounts are allowed between $5 and $50. Both bounds are stated on the send form itself, so customers see the limits before they run into them rather than after. There is deliberately no $1 tip: a dollar tip costs more to process than it returns, so the smallest send is $5.

Leaf a Tip's entire revenue is the Low Fives attached to each pack purchase — one $1 Low Five per $5 of credit, or 20% of credit value. Nothing is taken from the creator's side and there is no per-tip fee. The rate is proportional rather than flat so that percentage-based processing costs are covered at every pack size, not just the smallest one.

Customers hold a prepaid balance. Money is collected at pack purchase and spent later, so Leaf a Tip holds customer funds between those two events. Balances do not expire, are not transferable between accounts, and cannot be withdrawn as cash — they can only be spent as tips inside the platform. Typical balances are small, in the $5–$20 range, and most are spent within days of purchase.
This is a split-payment / marketplace model. Tips route from Leaf a Tip to individual creators, which requires a platform/marketplace payment structure — each creator needs their own payout destination (e.g., a Stripe Connect–style sub-account) rather than funds settling into a single merchant account. The Low Fives are the only portion that stays with Leaf a Tip.

04Who's Paying and Who's Receiving

Payers: Adult customers of cannabis-adjacent service businesses — tour attendees, dispensary customers, event guests. All 21+, since the businesses themselves are age-gated.

Recipients: The service providers and businesses using Leaf a Tip. Launch clients are in the Kansas City, Missouri market, starting with cannabis education and tour operators.

05Expected Volume

This is a new platform, so these are projections, not history:

  • Months 1–3: $500–$2,000/month in gross tip volume, single-digit business count
  • Months 4–12: $2,000–$10,000/month as more KC-area businesses onboard
  • Average card transaction: $6–$24 — a pack purchase. Individual tips draw down existing credit and are not card transactions.
  • Card transaction count: one per pack purchase, so materially fewer than the tip count, since a single pack funds several tips.

Growth is onboarding-driven — each new business brings its own customer base. No paid customer acquisition is planned for year one; businesses display the QR codes and their customers do the rest.

06Why the Chargeback Risk Is Low

  • Tips are voluntary and customer-initiated. Nobody disputes a tip they chose to leave five minutes ago the way they dispute a product that never arrived.
  • Nothing ships. Credit is delivered instantly and automatically the moment the payment clears.
  • Small ticket sizes. A $6 to $24 chargeback isn't worth most people's time.
  • No recurring billing, no free trials, no negative-option anything.
  • On the prepaid gap, stated plainly: a customer could in principle buy credit and dispute it before spending any. Balances are small and short-lived, every purchase is tied to an authenticated account, and each pack, send, and payout is recorded in a timestamped ledger.

07Compliance Posture

  • No cannabis, hemp, or CBD product is sold, marketed, or fulfilled through the platform.
  • No health or therapeutic claims appear anywhere on the site.
  • Recipient businesses are legal, licensed, or license-exempt operations in their jurisdictions (education and tour services).
  • Age verification is handled at the business level; Leaf a Tip's client businesses already gate at 21+.

08Where Liability Sits

Leaf a Tip processes tips only — a voluntary payment for service already rendered, initiated entirely by the customer, with no product or service purchased through Leaf a Tip itself. If a customer has an issue with the underlying product or service (a tour, a class, a retail purchase), that dispute is between the customer and the business that sold it, through that business's own payment processor. Leaf a Tip is not a party to that transaction and has no fulfillment obligation to dispute.

This keeps Leaf a Tip's own dispute exposure limited to the tip transaction itself, which is inherently low-risk for the reasons above.

09The Company

Leaf a Tip operates under TYPICAL LLC (Missouri). I build and maintain the platform myself — I'm a developer with a stack across GitHub, Firebase, and Cloudflare, and I already do web and payments-adjacent work for cannabis education businesses in the KC market, which is where the idea came from.

The guides kept getting asked "can I tip you?" and the answer was a shrug and a Venmo handle. Leaf a Tip is the better answer.

10The Gap This Sits In

Most processors sort a cannabis-adjacent applicant into one of two buckets: decline on the keyword, or underwrite and price it as full cannabis retail. Leaf a Tip fits neither, and the mismatch is worth naming directly.

  • None of the regulated-product burden. No seed-to-sale tracking, no METRC integration, no per-transaction license verification, no certificates of analysis, no THC limits to police, no age-gated fulfillment, no product liability. The things that make cannabis retail genuinely expensive and complicated to underwrite do not exist here, because no product is ever sold.
  • None of the standard high-risk transaction patterns. No recurring billing, no free trials, no negative-option enrollment, no subscriptions, no delivery window that can be missed. The dispute surface is a single voluntary, customer-initiated, low-dollar payment for service already rendered.
  • What it does carry is the association. The name, the customer base, the vertical. That is precisely the part automated aggregator screening reacts to on sight — and precisely the part a human underwriter is equipped to actually assess.

The net effect is an account whose real risk profile resembles a small tipping app, but which keyword screening files alongside dispensaries. Stripe, Square, and PayPal will decline or later freeze it on the association alone, without ever reaching the distinction.

For a processor willing to underwrite that distinction, this is a position rather than a concession. The cannabis-adjacent service sector — tours, education, events, hospitality — has the same tipping culture as any service industry and effectively no compliant tipping infrastructure, because the mainstream tools reject the category before evaluating it. A processor that supports one credible, clean-running platform here becomes the natural referral for the rest of the vertical as it formalizes.

To be clear about what this is and isn't: the volume projections in section 05 are deliberately conservative and I'm not asking anyone to price against a forecast. The argument here is about category position, not year-one revenue. I'd rather state the opportunity plainly up front than have it come up later as something I left out.
